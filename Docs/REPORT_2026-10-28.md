# Session report: 2026-10-28 (voxel floor, geology, biomes, breaking)

Everything below is committed on `main`. Nothing was verified inside Unity Play: Unity was in Play mode the
whole time, and the project rule is not to drive Play without asking. **Stop Play once.** Unity then
recompiles. Press Play again, and use the checklist at the end.

## 1. Voxel step ground (merged cubes + shader colour)
- New `VoxelStepSurface` builds terrain as real flat tops and vertical step walls. Neighbouring cells of the
  same height merge into one rectangle. Colour never splits geometry: each tile carries a small texture of
  10 cm cell colours (plus material and wetness) that the ground shader paints back on.
- Detail stays smooth with distance. Each level stores its parent heights, and the shader morphs every vertex
  toward the parent level (10 cm → 20 → 40 cm …) by on-screen size, so there is no popping between levels.
- Measured on the real spawn tiles (3×3), compared with the old per-pigment voxel faces:
  - Faces: 742k → 398k.
  - Memory: 86 → 35.7 MiB, textures included.
- 24 engine-free checks pass, including exact coverage oracles and a mirror of the shader's lookup maths.
- F1 has a **Voxel step ground** toggle (on by default; needs a Play restart) and a live
  **Smooth block-size transitions** toggle, for A/B comparison against the smooth surface.

## 2. Mountain materials (Geology v2)
- Rock bands are no longer periodic. Formations (~60 m) and beds (3–9 m) have hashed, irregular thicknesses.
  They dip and fold, and fault blocks offset them.
- Formations inherit a regional palette, so neighbouring beds are related tones instead of contrasting stripes.
  Crystalline basement takes over above roughly 2300 m.
- Weathering is layered on top: joints, exfoliation, patina, vertical streaks, lichen and desert varnish.
- Autoshader-style slope rules: the rock and scree cut-offs vary with noise and curvature. This adds outcrops
  and meadow pockets, and removes the single hard slope line.
- Before/after renders are in `Docs/GeologyV2/`. 44 surface checks pass.

## 4. Breaking: faster first hit, and moving on without undoing
Before, there was one session at a time. The next specimen could not start until you pressed O or walked
24 m away, and both of those **undid** the first one. Becoming breakable also waited on a full rebuild of all
vegetation matrices, about 0.5–2 s.

Now:
- **Instant handover.** When matter is ready, the original is cut out of the batches already on screen in the
  same frame. The full rebuild still runs in the background, but it no longer gates the first cut.
- **Template cache.** Up to 24 replays (3M cells) stay cached instead of one. While you walk, the nearest
  breakable species within 24 m are pre-generated in the background, and a punch never waits for that
  warm-up. Previously each species change regenerated the tree (seconds for big trees).
- **Taps are not lost.** A tap during preparation is queued (3 s) and performed when ready.
- **Move on.**
  - Punch a different tree, rock or plant and the current broken specimen is kept, not undone. Its matter
    keeps settling for up to 15 s, then freezes as a static mesh. The new one starts at once.
  - Walking more than 18 m away also keeps it.
  - Kept specimens stay hidden (draw, collision and aim) for the rest of the session. Inventory from kept
    specimens is preserved.
  - Up to 48 are kept nearby, within 1.5M cells. Older ones are retired: the original stays gone, and the
    remains disappear.
- **Looking around is cheaper.** Speculatively prepared matter no longer hides the original until you punch.
  A specimen you glance past is dropped after 0.6 s, without a rebuild.
- O still undoes the **current** specimen only.

Known limits:
- Kept remains have no collision, so you can walk through a stump.
- Mature redwoods with grounded roots vanish when you move on instead of staying.
- Lab plants (IDs 300+) are not breakable yet: they need replay recipes.
